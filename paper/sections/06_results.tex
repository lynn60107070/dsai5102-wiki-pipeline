\section{Results and Insights}\label{sec:results}

The Metabase dashboard \emph{Wikipedia edits} answers the question with eight tiles
(\cref{fig:dashboard} in the appendix): live edits per minute, edits per hour and per hour of day by bots and humans,
the bot share of the 15 busiest wikis, the top Wikipedia languages split into bots and humans, the share of
spikes followed by edits against other busy articles, the largest spikes, and data completeness per hour. Each tile has a title and a one-line explanation;
the \emph{From} and \emph{To} filters apply to every warehouse tile and the \emph{Project} filter to the two
tiles over all wikis. Over the window (28 Oct -- 11 Nov 2026, UTC), 8{,}479{,}562 of the 25{,}093{,}717
edits on 771 wikis (\cref{tab:profile}) were on 347 Wikipedias. We report five findings; the queries behind
every number are in \texttt{analysis/findings.sql} in the repository.

\paragraph{1. About half of all edits come from bots.} Flagged bot accounts made 48.6\% of all edits in
the window and 45.3\% of the edits on Wikipedias. The share moves from day to day, from 40.4\% (6 Nov)
to 61.2\% (1 Nov), when bots made 1.32 million edits, about 60\% more than on a typical day
(tile \emph{Edits per hour: bots vs humans}). For patrollers, about half of the raw edit count is work
that is hidden from their default review list.

\paragraph{2. Edit counts overstate small Wikipedias; human edits do not.} The bot share differs widely
by language (\cref{tab:wiki-ranks}). It is 30.9\% on English Wikipedia, 20.6\% on German and 12.9\% on
Spanish, but 81.6\% on Arabic and 99.5\% on Chechen. Chechen Wikipedia is second of all 347 Wikipedias by
edits, but 65th by human edits; Arabic falls from 5th to 17th (\cref{fig:dashboard-wikis}; tiles \emph{Bot share of edits} and \emph{Top languages on Wikipedia}). Ranking wikis by total edits would send review effort to wikis where
bots do almost all the work.

\paragraph{3. Human editing pressure is concentrated in a few Wikipedias.} English Wikipedia alone has
40.9\% of all human edits on Wikipedias; English, German and French together have 53.4\%, and the top 10
of 347 have 74.2\%. Outside Wikipedia, Wikidata and Wikimedia Commons have more edits than all Wikipedias
together (52.4\% of all edits, against 33.8\%), most of them structured data and file uploads.

\paragraph{4. Humans follow the clock, bots do not.} On Wikipedias, human edits average 16{,}400 an hour at
their peak at 17:00 UTC and 7{,}900 at their low at 04:00 UTC, a factor of 2.1; the six hours from 13:00
to 18:00 UTC carry 30.8\% of the day's human edits. Bot edits vary by a factor of 1.5 over the day
(tile \emph{Wikipedia edits by hour of day}). The human workload for patrollers therefore peaks between
13:00 and 18:00 UTC, the European afternoon and the American morning.

\paragraph{5. Most attention spikes are not followed by edits; very large ones are.} The window has
24{,}325 attention spikes (\cref{sec:data}), mostly on English (10{,}738), Japanese (3{,}768) and German
(2{,}677) Wikipedia. 77.6\% of them have no edit in the spike hour or the two hours after it. Overall,
spiking articles get about as many edits in the next two hours as other articles with at least 1{,}000
views in the hour (0.64 against 0.61 on average). The difference appears only for the largest spikes:
above 20{,}000 views, the 346 spikes got 5.5 edits in the next two hours against 1.8 for other article-hours
above 20{,}000 views that are not spikes, 3.1 times as many, and 57\% of them got at least one edit, against 22\% (tile \emph{Do attention spikes bring edits?}). The largest
surge followed breaking news: \emph{2026 Kraków shooting} on English Wikipedia went from about 45 views an
hour to thousands on 7 Nov and got 203 edits in the two hours after 16:00 UTC (tile \emph{Reader attention
spikes and the edits that follow}).
