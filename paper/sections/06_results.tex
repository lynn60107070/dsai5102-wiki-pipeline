\section{Results and Insights}\label{sec:results}

The Metabase dashboard \emph{Wikipedia edits} answers the question with eight tiles
(\cref{fig:dashboard} in the appendix): live edits per minute, edits per hour and per hour of day by bots and humans,
the bot share of the 15 busiest wikis, the top Wikipedia languages split into bots and humans, the share of
spikes followed by edits against steadily popular articles, the largest spikes, and data completeness per hour. Each tile has a title and a one-line explanation;
the \emph{From} and \emph{To} filters apply to every warehouse tile and the \emph{Project} filter to the two
tiles over all wikis. Over the window (28 Oct -- 11 Nov 2026, UTC), 8{,}479{,}562 of the 25{,}093{,}717
edits on 771 wikis (\cref{tab:profile}) were on 347 Wikipedias. We report five findings; the queries behind
every number are in \texttt{analysis/findings.sql} in the repository. They need the collected window,
which is not in the repository (only six sample hours are); on a fresh install they run on the sample.

\paragraph{1. About half of all edits come from bots.} Flagged bot accounts made 48.6\% of all edits in
the window and 45.3\% of the edits on Wikipedias. The share moves from day to day, from 40.4\% (6 Nov)
to 61.2\% (1 Nov), when bots made 1.32 million edits, about 60\% more than on bots' median day (0.81 million)
(tile \emph{Edits per hour: bots vs humans}). For patrollers, about half of the raw edit count is work
that is hidden from their default review list.

\paragraph{2. Total edit counts overstate bot-heavy Wikipedias.} The bot share differs widely
by language (\cref{tab:wiki-ranks}). It is 30.9\% on English Wikipedia, 20.6\% on German and 12.9\% on
Spanish, but 81.6\% on Arabic and 99.5\% on Chechen. Chechen Wikipedia is second of all 347 Wikipedias by
edits, but 65th by human edits; Arabic falls from 5th to 17th (\cref{fig:dashboard-wikis}; tiles \emph{Bot share of edits} and \emph{Top languages on Wikipedia}). Ranking wikis by total edits would send review effort to wikis where
bots do almost all the work.

\paragraph{3. Human editing pressure is concentrated in a few Wikipedias.} English Wikipedia alone has
40.9\% of all human edits on Wikipedias; English, German and French together have 53.4\%, and the top 10
of 347 have 74.2\%. Outside Wikipedia, Wikidata and Wikimedia Commons have more edits than all Wikipedias
together (52.4\% of all edits, against 33.8\%); these two projects hold structured data and media files,
not articles.

\paragraph{4. Humans follow the clock; bots far less.} On Wikipedias, human edits average 16{,}400 an hour at
their peak at 17:00 UTC and 7{,}900 at their low at 04:00 UTC, a factor of 2.1, and stay above 14{,}800
from 13:00 to 22:00 UTC: a broad plateau, not a sharp peak (the busiest six hours carry 31\%, against 25\%
if spread evenly). Bot edits vary by a factor of 1.5 over the day (tile \emph{Wikipedia edits by hour of
day}). Patrollers' human workload is highest in the European afternoon and evening and American morning.

\paragraph{5. Attention spikes are not an early sign of edit surges.} The window has 24{,}234
attention spikes on articles (\cref{sec:data}), mostly on English (10{,}719), Japanese (3{,}767) and German
(2{,}670) Wikipedia. 77.5\% of them have no edit in the spike hour or the two hours after it (0--2\,h).
Compared with article-hours that have as many views but no jump (steadily popular articles), spikes are
followed by an edit in the two hours after the spike hour (1--2\,h) \emph{less} often at every level of
views: 14\%
against 20\% of hours at 1{,}000--5{,}000 views, 32\% against 48\% at 5{,}000--20{,}000, and 57\% against
84\% above 20{,}000 (in that band, 346 spikes against 580 hours of 39 articles; tile \emph{Do attention spikes bring
edits?}). Counting the spike hour too does not change the direction (20\% against 26\%, 42\% against
57\%, 68\% against 90\%). Main pages and non-article pages such as
\emph{Special:Search} are left out: they get huge views and almost no edits, and when included made spikes
wrongly look three times as edited (\cref{tab:incidents}). Big news can still bring a burst of edits: \emph{2026 Kraków shooting} on English Wikipedia went from about 45 views an
hour to thousands on 7 Nov and got 203 edits in the two hours after 16:00 UTC (tile \emph{Reader attention
spikes and the edits that follow}), but a jump in readers alone is a weak sign of edits.
