\section{Data Sources}\label{sec:data}

We combine three public Wikimedia sources (\cref{tab:sources}). They differ in how they arrive, which
is why the pipeline has both a stream path and a batch path. All times are UTC.

\paragraph{Edits (stream).} Wikimedia EventStreams~\cite{wikimedia2026eventstreams} publishes every change
on every Wikimedia wiki as a server-sent event, within seconds. We read the \emph{recentchange} feed
over HTTPS and keep edits and page creations, about half of all events; logs and category changes are
dropped. Each event names the wiki, the page, the time, whether the account has the bot flag, and the
user name. The raw archive keeps events as received; the clean zone, which the warehouse reads, keeps
only a hash of the user name. The feed covers every Wikimedia project, so the volumes in
\cref{tab:sources,tab:profile} are for all of them, and the bot share is also given for the Wikipedias
alone. There is no archive to download later, so the collector runs all the time and replays outages
from the feed. Licence: none is stated for the event data itself; its use falls under the Wikimedia
Terms of Use~\cite{wikimedia2026terms}.

\paragraph{Reader attention (batch).} Wikimedia publishes the views of every page as one compressed file
per hour~\cite{wikimedia2026pageviews}, counting human readers only. We keep the articles with at least
10 views in the hour, plus the total views per wiki over all articles. We call an article-hour an
\emph{attention spike} when it has at least 1{,}000 views and at least five times the article's average
over the previous 24 hours, so the 10-view cut-off keeps every spike. The files stay online, so missed
hours can be loaded later. Licence: CC0~\cite{wikimedia2026dumpslegal}.

\paragraph{Wiki names (reference).} The Sitematrix API~\cite{wikimedia2026sitematrix} lists every
Wikimedia wiki with its project and language. It changes rarely, so it is read once and stored as a small
reference table that turns ids like \texttt{arzwiki} into ``Egyptian Arabic Wikipedia''.
Licence: none is stated for the API output; as for the stream, the Terms of Use apply.

\paragraph{Data profile.} \Cref{tab:profile} summarises the data collected in the analysis window, 28 Oct
to 11 Nov 2026 (UTC; 15 days, 360 hours). The pipeline kept 25{,}093{,}717 edits and page creations, about
1.67 million a day, on 771 wikis. Edits by flagged bot accounts made up 48.6\% of all edits
and 45.3\% of edits on the Wikipedias. Because the feed is delivered at least once and outages were
replayed, 10.8 million events, 30\% of the raw zone, arrived more than once; they are removed by compaction in the lake and
then by event id in dbt, before the warehouse tables. Outages were replayed (\cref{sec:implementation}),
so no hour of the window is empty. Four hours, 02:00--06:00 UTC on 28 Oct, have under half the median
hour's edits; bot and human edits both fell, at 03:00 UTC to 6\% and 16\% of the median hour. The raw
zone has no more events for those hours, so the drop is in what the feed delivered, not a loss in the
pipeline.
They are kept and marked on the dashboard's completeness tile. Pageview files are missing for 13 hours,
so no attention spike can be found in those hours.
